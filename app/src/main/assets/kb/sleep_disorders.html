Types of Sleep Disorders

Insomnia

  • One of the most common disorders in the U.S.
  • Types :
    • Transient - temporary (lasts for less than a week)
    • Acute - lasts for a few weeks
    • Chronic - lasts for longer than a month
  • Patterns :
    • Onset - difficulty falling asleep (may be associated with anxiety disorders)
    • Middle - waking in the middle of the night, difficulty falling back asleep and maintaining consistent sleep
    • Terminal - waking earlier in the morning (may be associated with clinical depression)
  • Symptoms :
    • Difficulty sleeping with any of the above patterns
    • Feeling tired/falling asleep throughout the day
    • Waking up feeling fatigued
    • Impaired focus, memory, coordination

Sleep Apnea

  • Common condition characterized by pauses in breathing when sleeping.
  • Types :
    • Obstructive - most common, caused by obstruction in airway
    • Central - brain sends incorrect signals to respiratory system
  • Symptoms :
    • Severe, chronic snoring or dry mouth/throat
    • Feeling tired/falling asleep throughout the day
    • Waking up feeling fatigued
    • Impaired focus, memory, coordination

Restless Legs Syndrome

  • Neurological disorder characterized by irresistible urge to move
  • Symptoms :
    • Need to move parts of body in order to stop or avoid uncomfortable sensations
    • Most commonly affects legs but may also affect arms or torso
    • Starts or becomes more severe when inactive
    • Makes it difficult to fall or stay asleep
    • May also show symptoms for Periodic Limb Movements

Narcolepsy

  • Narcolepsics have irregular sleep patterns and may fall asleep abruptly at inappropriate times or settings
  • Symptoms :
    • Excessive daytime sleepiness, especially after sufficient sleep
    • Cataplexy (sudden loss of muscle control)
    • Sleep paralysis (when mind wakes but body is still “asleep”)
    • Hypnagogia (vivid hallucinations during sleep-wake transitions)
    • Automatic behavior (i.e. sleepwalking, sleeptalking)

Hypersomnia

  • Excessive or daytime sleepiness
  • Types :
    • Primary - chronic excessive daytime sleepiness (EDS)
    • Recurrent - recurring EDS with periods of no symptoms
  • Symptoms :
    • May experience “sleep attacks” at inappropriate times
    • Need to nap constantly throughout the day
    • Difficulty waking up, sometimes even after full sleep
    • Impaired focus, memory, coordination

Parasomnias

  • Sleepwalking - walking or doing other routine activities while unconscious
  • Bruxism - teeth grinding or clenching during sleep
  • Night Terrors - episodes of frightening behavior/hallucinations while unconscious
  • REM Behavior Disorder - when sleepers actively act out their dreams because muscles were not paralyzed during sleep, potentially dangerous for themselves and for others around them
